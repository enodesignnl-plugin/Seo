Two ways to write
a garden.

In Dari, rawza (روضه) means garden — the walled, watered paradise garden of Afghan and Persian tradition. This document presents two genuinely different typographic identities for a premium Afghan restaurant in Apeldoorn, drawn as custom vector letterforms and tested on a menu, a façade, a website and a phone.

Reading the references

Nine images were supplied: the restaurant's own logo experiments and a set of hospitality wordmarks the client likes. We treated them as evidence of taste, not as templates. What follows is what they say — and what they do not.

  1. Typography the client is drawn to

    High-contrast display capitals with sharp hairlines (02, 06, 09), wide classical caps with generous tracking (01), and small spaced subtitles in a quieter face. Every favourite is set in capitals or a single decisive capital. Nothing is script, nothing is bold.

  2. How the wordmarks customise letters

    Each one changes one or two letters and leaves the rest alone: The Palu replaces the A's crossbar with a bar above it, Mazar removes it, Puerto detaches the R's leg, Kaaj ligates its double A, the second RAWZA experiment swings the R into a long swash. The lesson: one memorable letter beats five decorated ones.

  3. Connections worth exploring

    RAWZA has a natural rhythm of diagonals — A W Z A — and an R at the start that can lead into it. The R→A junction and the W at the centre of the word are the two places where a connection or a distinctive form will be felt most.

  4. What reads as exclusive

    Restraint, space and finish: a small mark on a large, textured field (02, 06), foil or emboss rather than printed gradients, hairline rules, tiny subtitles. The weaker references (03, 08) lose exclusivity through generic leaf and arch clip-art and centred-everything layouts.

  5. Colour and material

    Warm darks — chocolate, burgundy, deep green — carrying a champagne or brass metal, plus warm ivory paper. The blue in the existing experiment (01) is worth keeping as a thought: lapis lazuli, mined in Badakhshan for thousands of years, is one of Afghanistan's most distinctive colours.

  6. Logo vs. interior and web

    Logo references: 01, 02, 04, 06, 09 (letter construction, hierarchy, finish). Interior and web references: 05 (dark photography under a light wordmark), 02 and 06 (paper and texture), 07 (tone-on-tone backgrounds). 03 and 08 mainly tell us what to move away from.

Not yet received

The brief mentions interior photographs, fabric and material samples and a written client briefing. Only the nine logo references above were supplied. The palettes below are therefore derived from those references and the described interior — warm wood, rich fabrics, chandeliers — and should be checked against physical samples on site.

Spelling

The green experiment (03) reads “AFGAANS”. The Dutch spelling is Afghaans — “AFGHAANS RESTAURANT” is used throughout this document.

Capitalisation

Why both directions are set in capitals

Title case and lower case were sketched in a stock serif before any drawing began. Rawza reads friendly but café-like, and puts the visual weight on a single R; rawza is contemporary but loses authority on a façade and is the form most often seen in fast-casual branding. Capitals give five letters of equal rank — R A W Z A — which is exactly the symmetry both concepts exploit: a W at the centre, an A on either side of it.

The two lower rows are typeset sketches in Cormorant Garamond, shown for comparison only. They are not custom letterforms.

Concept A

The Signature

The whole identity lives in five capitals. The luxury comes from drawing, spacing and restraint.

RAWZA is written the way a host signs a guest book: once, with care. The proportions are wide and calm, taken from inscriptional Roman capitals; the contrast is sharp and editorial, like a well-printed menu. There is no emblem. Three letters carry the signature and everything else stays quiet, so the mark works in one colour on a menu, in brass on a wall and in a browser tab.

The Afghan character is in the gesture rather than in ornament. The R's leg runs past the baseline and stretches into a long hairline beneath the A — a Latin translation of the kashida, the horizontal extension Persian calligraphers use to give a word breath and emphasis. It is the one flourish in the identity, and it reads as a signature.

  • R A leg that becomes a hairline tail and ends on the axis of the following A.
  • W Two interlocking Vs. The crossing sits at the centre of the word.
  • A Pointed apex, low hairline bar. Both A's stand tall and frame the W.
A·01

Primary logo

Champagne on walnut. Subtitle in Cormorant Garamond Medium caps, tracked +420, at 9% of the wordmark's cap height. Recommendation: “Afghaans restaurant” — it states the cuisine without adding a symbol, and its length (60% of the wordmark) settles the tail of the R. “Restaurant” alone is cleaner but leaves the composition top-heavy.

A·02

Alternative logo

Stacked signature & monogram

The signature R on its own becomes the monogram: wax seals, napkin embroidery, the brass door push, the favicon. In the stacked version it sits above a hairline rule, the wordmark and the subtitle — for square formats such as the menu cover, social avatars and gift vouchers.

No new elements are introduced. The alternative is the same letter, given more room.

A·03

Light and dark versions

Shown with the small-size lockup (A·09), which is what these widths call for. The mark is a single flat colour in every version. It needs no gradient, outline or shadow to hold up — the hairlines and the tail carry it.

A·04

Typography details

Drawn from scratch as vector outlines on a 700-unit cap height: thick strokes 88 units, hairlines 16 (contrast 5.5 : 1), bracketed serifs 44 units wide. No typeface was modified — the closest relatives are inscriptional capitals such as Trajan and the display cuts of Didot, but the letters below exist only for RAWZA.

R — the signature leg

  1. The leg leaves the bowl at the classical angle and keeps its full weight.
  2. It passes through the baseline without a foot serif.
  3. It turns and thins into a hairline that runs 10% below the baseline, under the A's left foot — the kashida gesture.
  4. The tail ends exactly on the axis of the A's apex, so the flourish is anchored in the construction rather than drawn freehand.

W — crossed centre

  1. Two interlocking Vs: the hairline of the first crosses the heavy stroke of the second.
  2. Each stroke keeps its own serif; a notch separates them at the cap line.
  3. Pointed vertices overshoot the baseline by 16 units so they look level.

W is the middle letter of RAWZA. The crossing puts a quiet knot at the centre of the word.

A — pointed and tall

  1. Sharp apex with an 18-unit overshoot above the cap line.
  2. Hairline crossbar set at 35% of the height, lower than usual: the counter becomes a tall, quiet triangle.
  3. Short cupped brackets — inscriptional rather than Bodoni-sharp.

Z — kept plain

  1. Beaked terminals answer the bracketed serifs.
  2. The diagonal at full weight continues the A–W–Z–A rhythm.

Deliberately unmodified: a decorated Z would compete with the R.

Spacing is set by eye on the counters, not by metrics: R–A +66, A–W −34, W–Z +44, Z–A +6 units. The two A's and the crossbar line make the word symmetrical around the W.

A·09

Small-scale test

RAWZA — Afghaans restaurant

Honest assessment

  • Display cut still reads at 16 px cap height, but below about 24 px its 16-unit hairlines drop under one device pixel on a standard screen: crossbars, serifs and the W's thin strokes turn faint grey and the word loses its sparkle. Under 12 px the crossbars disappear.
  • Small cut (hairlines 30, stems 98, +18 tracking) stays readable down to about 9–10 px cap height — enough for a mobile header (18–22 px) and email signatures. Use it for everything under 40 px.
  • The R's tail survives to about 12 px; below that it becomes a smudge under the A. Acceptable — the word still reads as RAWZA without it.
  • At 16 px the monogram R still reads, with the tail reduced to a faint hairline. A pixel-hinted favicon version should be drawn in Illustrator — flagged for the next round.
Concept B

Contemporary Heritage

Afghan architecture and the garden plan, translated into letters and one restrained emblem.

The letters are tall, condensed and glyphic: no serifs, a gentle flare at the feet, moderate contrast. Both A's are drawn as pointed four-centred arches standing on slightly splayed piers — the arch of Timurid and Afghan gateways from Herat to Mazar-i-Sharif — and their crossbars sit like impost lines just below the springing. The W's centre peak stops short of the cap line so the two arches lead the rhythm.

The emblem is a plan, not a picture: a chahar bagh, the walled four-part garden of Persian and Mughal tradition — Babur, who laid out gardens in this manner, is buried in one in Kabul. It is reduced to a wall, four beds whose corners open an octagonal court, and an eight-point pool in lapis. It literally draws the name: rawza, garden.

  • A Pointed four-centred arch on piers; crossbar as impost line.
  • W Lowered centre peak, so the arches dominate the word.
  • Garden-plan emblem — the only colour accent is the lapis pool.
B·01

Primary logo

Ivory and brass on cypress green. Subtitle in Jost Regular caps, tracked +500. Recommendation: “Afghaans restaurant”. Here the emblem already carries the heritage, so the shorter “Restaurant” is a valid alternative if the client prefers a quieter line — this concept is less dependent on the subtitle than A.

B·02

Alternative logo

Horizontal seal, emblem, pattern

The horizontal lockup sets the emblem as a seal beside the wordmark — the format for the website header, the fascia and the letterhead. The emblem alone works as a stamp, a coaster and a social avatar, and tiled tone-on-tone it becomes a pattern for menu endpapers, bags and upholstery piping.

B·03

Light and dark versions

Shown with the small-size lockup (B·09). The lapis pool is the only second colour and is optional: in one-colour reproduction the star simply takes the logo colour. On dark grounds it switches to brass, because lapis on cypress has too little contrast (1.4 : 1).

B·04

Typography details

Drawn on the same 700-unit cap height as Concept A but on a different skeleton: condensed proportions (the A is 372 units wide against 644 in Concept A), main strokes 58, secondary strokes 28 (2 : 1), no serifs. The flare at the feet is a concave curve 9 units wide over 84 units of height — enough to soften the ends, not enough to read as serifs.

A — the arch

  1. Pointed four-centred arch: tight shoulders, long straight-ish approach to the point. The apex overshoots by 18 units.
  2. The arch springs at 58% of the cap height; piers below are near-vertical.
  3. The crossbar is placed like an impost line, just below the springing, so the letter still reads as A.
  4. Piers splay outward by 9 units — a hint of an A's diagonals — and end in a glyphic flare.

W — lowered peak

  1. The centre peak stops at 79% of the cap height, leaving a gap at the top of the word between the two arches.
  2. Pointed feet with a 10-unit overshoot; heavy down-strokes, light up-strokes.

Emblem — the garden plan

  1. The wall.
  2. Four beds: the classical quadripartite garden.
  3. Chamfered inner corners open an octagonal court.
  4. The pool, drawn as an eight-point star (khatam) in lapis.
  5. Hairline water channels — dropped in the small version.

The springing line runs through both arches and the bowl of the R; the lowered W peak leaves the cap line open between them. R and Z are kept plain on purpose.

B·09

Small-scale test

RAWZA — Afghaans restaurant

Honest assessment

  • Display cut reads down to about 12 px cap height — condensed letters with 2 : 1 contrast are sturdier than Concept A's hairlines, though the crossbars of the A thin out below 16 px.
  • Small cut (secondary strokes 40, +26 tracking) stays readable to about 8–9 px cap height. The pointed arches are still recognisable at 12 px.
  • The emblem with water channels needs about 64 px; below that the channels fill in. The small emblem (heavier wall, no channels) reads at 16 px as a square with a star, which is enough for a favicon.
  • Condensed letters lose width quickly: in a narrow mobile header the wordmark gets small before it gets illegible, so it pairs best with the emblem beside it.

Recommendation

Concept A is the stronger answer to the brief as written: the client's priority is unique, recognisable typography, and A is the more ownable piece of drawing — nobody else's R does what this R does. It also fits the described interior of wood, fabric and chandeliers more naturally. Concept B is the better choice if the restaurant wants its Afghan identity legible at a glance from the street, or plans a lighter, more contemporary interior. We do not recommend combining them: the arch A and the signature R come from different skeletons and would weaken each other.

Next round — in Illustrator
  1. Redraw outlines with clean node placement and extrema; adjust optical overshoots by eye at print size.
  2. Fine-tune kerning of all four pairs at 3 sizes; finalise the small cut and a hinted 16 px favicon.
  3. Decide the subtitle typeface licence. Cormorant Garamond and Jost (SIL Open Font License) were used here and are free for commercial use; a commercial alternative can be swapped in.
  4. Proof foil, blind deboss and brass letter samples; check palette against the actual upholstery and wood.
  5. Trademark and domain check for the chosen wordmark before roll-out.
